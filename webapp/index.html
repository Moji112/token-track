How to size up a coin before you buy anything
  • Check that liquidity is meaningfully larger than what you'd risk — and isn't just one wallet's temporary deposit.
  • Open the token on the chain's block explorer and look at holder distribution. If one or two wallets hold most of the supply, they can crash the price whenever they want. Watch out for the reverse trick too: a huge holder count can be faked by mass-airdropping tokens to random wallets, so a big number alone isn't proof of anything.
  • Check whether contract ownership was renounced or time-locked. An active owner can often mint more tokens, change fees, or disable selling entirely.
  • Read whether the contract is verified on the explorer rather than trusting the name and logo alone — anyone can copy those.
  • Search the project's name alongside "rug" or "scam" before putting in real money.
  • Treat "boosted" or "trending" labels as marketing, not vetting. Anyone can pay DexScreener to be featured — it says nothing about the team or the code.
  • A "Most held" listing just means many wallets hold it and it's been indexed by the explorer — it does not mean it has a working market. Check for a live trading pair before assuming you could actually sell it.
  • Start small. You can always add more once you've watched something for a few days.

How to size up an NFT collection before you buy
  • Confirm the collection's contract address using a marketplace's own verification badge or the chain's block explorer — never trust a link from a DM, comment, or random search result.
  • Never sign a transaction from an NFT you didn't mint yourself. A "surprise" airdropped NFT is one of the most common ways people get tricked into approving a wallet-draining contract.
  • Check the holder count and mint history. A collection where a handful of wallets hold most of the supply is easy for those wallets to manipulate — and a suspiciously huge holder count can mean mass airdrops, not real demand.
  • Compare the floor price across more than one marketplace source — a single low sale can make a floor look lower than what you'd actually pay to buy in.
  • Look up the creator's social accounts and any past projects before minting or buying.
